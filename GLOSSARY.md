# What the words mean

Three kinds of vocabulary appear in this product. Knowing which is which matters,
because two of them are the customer's language and one of them is the product's
argument.

## Real accounting and audit terms

These are what a Corporate Controller already says. They are not softened.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Workpaper** | An auditor's document for one item: the evidence gathered, the rule applied, the reasoning, the conclusion. Real audit vocabulary. Every decision here renders as one so an auditor can pick it up cold. |
| **Disallowed** | The charge is not reimbursable. Standard in expense and tax work, and stronger than "rejected", which sounds like a workflow state rather than a finding. |
| **Journal entry** | The double-entry record posted to the ledger: debits on one side, credits on the other, equal to the paisa. |
| **Trial balance** | Every account's debits and credits totalled. If the two totals differ, something is wrong with the books. |
| **Cost centre** | Which part of the business bears the cost. Not always the employee's own department. |
| **Input tax credit** | Recoverable GST, where the vendor's registration number appears on the receipt. Money the company gets back, and usually does not bother to claim. |
| **Accrual** | Spend that has happened but has not been posted yet. The reason month end is a scramble. |
| **Precedent** | A past ruling that binds comparable cases. Borrowed from law, and used the same way here. |

## Plain words, chosen over jargon

Where the industry has a term but it obscures more than it carries.

| Used here | The industry word | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Decide | Adjudicate | "Adjudicate" is standard in insurance claims, not in T&E. "Decide" is clearer and more accurate about what actually happens. |
| Needs the Controller | Escalated | Says who has to do something. "Escalated" says only that a workflow moved. |
| Needs you | Escalation queue | Same reason, from the Controller's side of the screen. |
| Gather, Check, Decide, Authority, Post | The agent pipeline | Named for what each step does, not for the architecture. |

## Deliberate framing

These are the product's argument and are not standard anywhere. They are used only
where the surface itself explains them.

| Term | What it is doing |
|---|---|
| **Authority** (tab), *what she may do without asking* (panel) | The employment terms: approval limit, rejection limit, confidence floor, risk ceiling. Framed as terms rather than settings because that is the claim: an employee has authority, a tool has configuration. |
| **Rulings** (tab), *precedent book* (panel) | What the Controller taught her, generalised. The learning loop. |
| **Evaluation** | Ordinarily called an eval suite. Framed as a performance review because that is the decision it feeds: whether to widen her authority. |
| **Shadow, Suggest, Autonomous** | The deployment rungs. Standard in ML rollout, less so in finance software. |

## The name

The agent needs one, and a person's name reinforces "employee" where a function name
would reinforce "tool". Whatever it ends up as, check it first: the working name in this
build collides with the W3C accessibility standard and with an existing AI assistant,
which is exactly the mistake worth not making twice.
